About

Research centers on molecular-scale transport phenomena in nanoconfined and electrochemical systems, integrating molecular dynamics simulations, machine learning potentials, and continuum modeling. Emphasis is placed on ion and solvent transport under sub-nanometer confinement, electric double layer physics, electrokinetic processes, and nanoscale heat and mass transfer. The overarching goal is to establish predictive multi-scale frameworks that bridge atomistic mechanisms with continuum theory, enabling advances in semiconductor manufacturing, catalytic interfaces, energy conversion systems, and next-generation microfluidic technologies.

Research contributions

  • Electroosmotic flow and double layer structure — molecular simulation frameworks for electrokinetic transport in sub-1 nm nanochannels, showing how ion concentration, charge distribution, and confinement govern flow and local viscosity.
  • Nanodroplet wetting dynamics — atomistic modeling of ionic nanodroplets, quantifying how dissimilar molecular boundaries change contact angle and surface interaction.
  • Thermal transport at dissimilar interfaces — NEMD studies of where Fourier's law holds and where it breaks down, and the role of atomic-scale heterogeneity in thermal resistance.
  • Continuum-to-atomistic transition modeling — multiscale models joining statistical thermodynamics with classical continuum mechanics for transport under extreme confinement.
  • Research development — led proposals funded by the National Research Foundation of Korea on nanoscale semiconductor processes, high-density fuel cells, and interfacial energy transport.
